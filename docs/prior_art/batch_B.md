# Prior-art check, batch B (H001: certified online write-omission in grouped damped-oscillator SSMs)

Date of check: 2026-11-04. Method: WebFetch of arXiv abs/HTML pages (and ar5iv), the PMLR page, and WebSearch (standard mode). The extraction was done by a summarizing fetch model, not by reading the full text line by line myself. Equation and section numbers below are as that model reported them. Spot-check them against the PDFs before citing anything load-bearing.

H001 recap, used for the comparisons below. For each group i, the recurrence is h_{i,t} = A_{i,t} h_{i,t-1} + m_{i,t} b_{i,t}, with diagonal complex A and |a| <= 1. Before computing W_i u_t, the controller forms a cheap bound beta_{i,t} = |g_i| wbar_i ||u_t||. It keeps a scalar ledger e_{i,t} = rho_i e_{i,t-1} + (1 - m_{i,t}) beta_{i,t}, and a causal budget policy keeps sum_i c_i e_{i,t} <= epsilon, giving a per-step guarantee ||y_t - y_dense_t|| <= epsilon against the dense frozen model. The homogeneous dynamics always advance.

## 1. LAST: Layer-Adaptive State Pruning for Deep State Space Models

- **Authors:** Minseon Gwak, Seongrok Moon, Joohwan Ko, PooGyeon Park
- **Version/date:** arXiv:2411.02824. v1 2024-11-05, v2 2025-01-14, v3 2025-01-31 (inspected). NeurIPS 2024 per the arXiv comments.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02824 (HTML v3: https://arxiv.org/html/2411.02824v3)
- **Access level:** full-text inspected. HTML read through about 100k of 114k characters; the tail, which is late appendices and experiments, was not read.
- **Relevant parts:** Sec. 2.2 (diagonal complex S4D/S5, ZOH, Hurwitz stability); Eq. 5–6 (per-mode H-infinity score); Eq. 2–4 and App. B.1–B.2 (layer-level bound); Eq. 7–8 and App. B.3 (model-level bound through 1-Lipschitz GELU); Eq. 9–10 (LAST normalization).
- **Verified mechanism:** For a diagonal SSM, each state i is a subsystem with ||G_i||_inf = ||C_i B_i|| / (1 - |lambda_i|). The score is ||C_i||^2 ||B_i||^2 / (1 - |lambda_i|)^2. Appendix B derives an upper bound on the layer output perturbation, ||f(u;Sigma) - f(u;Sigma_-P)||_2^2 <= sum_{i in P} ||G_i||_inf^2 ||u||_2^2. The bound uses the triangle inequality over modes and a 1-Lipschitz activation, and a product over layers propagates it through the model. Pruning is static and parameter-only, and it removes the whole state (lambda_i, B row, C column) for all time steps. The paper says each score is "independently determined based on the trained parameters".
- **Overlap with H001:** Both use the same per-mode decomposition of a diagonal SSM and the same contraction-based gain for the omitted contribution. If H001 omits every write of a mode under worst-case input, its ledger reaches beta/(1 - rho), and c_i beta/(1 - rho) is the same kind of quantity as ||C_i|| ||B_i|| / (1 - |lambda_i|). Both bound output deviation from the unpruned frozen model, both use a triangle-inequality sum over modes, and both use a global ranking to decide what to drop under a budget.
- **Remaining difference:** LAST is offline, input-independent, and permanent. It removes states, so the homogeneous dynamics are removed too, and its bound is an ell_2 sequence-energy bound in terms of ||u||_2 over the whole sequence. It has no per-time-step decision, no causal ledger, no per-step ell_inf-in-time guarantee, no pre-write input-dependent bound, and no adaptive budget that recovers as the ledger decays. Speedup there comes from smaller state, not from skipped write matmuls.
- **What would eliminate the difference:** A LAST follow-up, or code, that applies the H-infinity or modal criterion per time step on the input-weighted write ||B_i u_t||. Equally, a version that keeps the states but masks writes online with a running bound.

## 2. AIRE-Prune: Asymptotic Impulse-Response Energy for State Pruning in State Space Models

- **Authors:** Apurba Prasad Padhy, Fernando Camacho, Saibal Mukhopadhyay
- **Version/date:** arXiv:2602.00534 v1, 2026-01-31. ICLR 2026 per the arXiv listing.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00534 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00534v1)
- **Access level:** full-text inspected (HTML, including App. A–C as reported).
- **Relevant parts:** Eq. 4 (DT diagonal layer); Eq. 7–8 (finite-horizon and infinite-horizon per-mode energy E_i = ||C_{:,i}||^2 ||B_{i,:}||^2 / (1 - |lambda_i|^2)); Eq. 9 (approximate additivity, without cross terms); Eq. 11 and Alg. 1 (prefix-normalized score, global threshold); **App. B.5, Eq. 12–17 (certificate)**; App. B.6, Eq. 18–19 (comparison with LAST); Table 3 and Table 5 (runtime).
- **Verified mechanism:** Each mode gets a closed-form, data-free asymptotic impulse-response energy score. Scores are prefix-normalized per layer, and the method keeps a contiguous prefix in each layer under a global state budget. **It does include a certificate.** App. B.5 bounds the layer transfer-function error from pruning a set T. It first bounds the error by sum_{i in T} ||G_i||_inf. Using ||G_i||_inf <= alpha_i / (1 - |lambda_i|), it then reaches the boxed bound epsilon <= kappa(rho) min{ sum sqrt(E_i), sqrt(|T|) sqrt(sum E_i) }, where kappa(rho) = sqrt((1 + rho) / (1 - rho)) and rho = max over the pruned modes of |lambda_i|. The certificate covers only the linear layer, and the promised end-to-end stack bound (App. B.1) was not found in the text. Pruning is static and offline. The paper reports physical-reduction speedups of 1.23x to 2.86x on H100 for S5 (Table 3), and Mamba-labelled results in Table 5 that are inconsistent with App. C.3, which transfers into S5.
- **Overlap with H001:** H001 uses a worst-case modal gain with rho = max |lambda| in a geometric factor 1 / (1 - rho). AIRE uses the same structure: alpha_i / (1 - |lambda_i|) with ||C|| ||B|| norms, plus a triangle sum over dropped modes. Both are certified budget-constrained selections of what to drop from a diagonal SSM, with a measured runtime benefit. AIRE's per-mode certificate is mathematically the infinite-horizon, worst-case-input limit of H001's ledger.
- **Remaining difference:** AIRE's certificate is a sup over frequencies, that is, an H-infinity bound on the operator for all inputs. H001's certificate is a realized, input-conditioned time-domain bound that is updated causally. AIRE drops modes entirely, including their homogeneous dynamics. H001 drops individual writes at individual steps and keeps the state, so a group that was omitted earlier can be written again later. AIRE's future work states that input-dependent models are not handled.
- **What would eliminate the difference:** Any AIRE variant or follow-up that evaluates the energy or H-infinity certificate per window or per step with the actual input, and gates writes against a running budget.

## 3. Quamba: A Post-Training Quantization Recipe for Selective State Space Models

- **Authors:** Hung-Yueh Chiang, Chi-Chih Chang, Natalia Frumkin, Kai-Chiang Wu, Diana Marculescu
- **Version/date:** arXiv:2410.13229. v1 2024-10-17, v2 2024-12-07 (inspected). No venue on the arXiv page. It is widely reported as ICLR 2025, but I did not verify that.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13229 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13229v2)
- **Access level:** full-text inspected (HTML in two passes, including App. N.2 and the end of App. K).
- **Relevant parts:** Sec. 4.1 and Thm. 4.1 (main text), restated as Thm. N.1 in App. N.2; Sec. 4.2 (percentile clipping of the SSM input, Hadamard transform on the output); App. B and Table 6 (dynamic quantization baseline); App. I (offline skip-quantization sensitivity); App. K and Table 11.
- **Verified mechanism:** This is static 8-bit per-tensor PTQ for Mamba. It clips the SSM input x at a calibrated percentile and quantizes the output y after a Walsh–Hadamard rotation that is fused into W_out. The error analysis models only input quantization error, ||delta_x(t)|| <= epsilon, in h(t) = A(t) h(t-1) + B x(t). The difference Delta(t) = A(t) Delta(t-1) + B delta_x(t) is unrolled and bounded geometrically, under the assumption ||A(t)||_2 <= a e^{t-T} with 0 < a < 1 and ||B|| <= b. This gives ||Delta(T)|| <= epsilon b / (1 - a). The paper describes the assumption on A as motivated by an observed decay, i.e. that the norm "can be bounded by an exponential function". It has no online budget and no adaptive skipping.
- **Overlap with H001:** The error recursion is structurally identical to H001's ledger. A per-step input-write perturbation is propagated by a contraction of the state transition, and a geometric sum bounds it. This is direct prior use of "write-perturbation ledger with contraction factor" reasoning in SSMs.
- **Remaining difference:** Quamba's bound is an a-priori analysis with a uniform epsilon and an empirically motivated, rather odd assumption on A. It is never maintained at run time and never used to choose computation. It bounds state error, not output error under a budget, and the perturbation is quantization noise, not omitted writes. Quamba gets its speedup from low precision, not from skipping.
- **What would eliminate the difference:** A Quamba follow-up (e.g., Quamba2) or another SSM PTQ work that tracks the per-step perturbation bound online and adapts precision or skipping per token to hold an output tolerance. I did not check Quamba2 here.

## 4. GHOST: Unmasking Phantom States in Mamba2 via Grouped Hidden-state Output-aware Selection & Truncation

- **Authors:** Michael Menezes, Anastasios Kyrillidis
- **Version/date:** arXiv:2602.11408 v1, 2026-02-11. ICML 2026, PMLR vol. 306, pp. 87805–87821.
- **URL:** https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/menezes26a.html (arXiv HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11408v1). The PMLR PDF URL I tried returned 404, so the methods were read from the arXiv HTML.
- **Access level:** full-text inspected (arXiv HTML v1).
- **Relevant parts:** Sec. 4.2–4.3 (balanced-truncation motivation); Eq. 4 (saliency S = sqrt(mean(H^2 · C'^2)) over calibration tokens, heads and channels); Sec. 4.4 (local loss proportional to S^2, assuming D = 0); Sec. 4.5 and Alg. 1 (grouping); Sec. 4.6 (complexity); Sec. 6 (limitations).
- **Verified mechanism:** GHOST approximates balanced truncation for Mamba2 from forward-pass statistics only. It uses the squared hidden-state activation as a controllability proxy and the squared instantaneous C as an observability proxy, and their product is the per-state saliency. Scores are pooled across all groups of a layer so that capacity is allocated adaptively per group. The masks are computed once from 128 calibration samples and applied as soft zero masks on W_B, W_C and the conv filters. The paper has no theorem or error bound. It reports no inference speedup, and it leaves physical speedup to future work. It covers Mamba2 and Zamba2 only, and says that extending to S4/H3/Mamba1 may require adapting the Gramian approximations.
- **Overlap with H001:** GHOST also allocates a budget by output-aware saliency per group, combining "how much is written" (controllability) with "how much is read" (observability, C). H001's c_i beta_{i,t} is the input-conditioned, worst-case analogue of that product. Both use a grouped structure.
- **Remaining difference:** GHOST is static, calibration-averaged and heuristic, with no guarantee. Its target is a selective (time-varying A, B, C) family rather than an LTI oscillator family. It removes state dimensions rather than per-step writes and reports no runtime gain.
- **What would eliminate the difference:** A GHOST variant that computes the controllability times observability product per token online and gates writes, with a bound. This is not present in the inspected text.

## 5. State dimension reduction of recurrent equilibrium networks with contraction and robustness preservation

- **Author:** Mohammad Fahim Shakib (single author)
- **Version/date:** arXiv:2508.02843 v1, 2025-08-04. No venue listed (eess.SY).
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02843 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02843v1)
- **Access level:** full-text inspected (HTML).
- **Relevant parts:** Sec. II-A, Eq. 1–2 (REN, slope-restricted activations); Thm. 1, Eq. 4 (contraction LMI with P); Eq. 5 (incremental IQC robustness); Sec. III-A, Eq. 6–8 (Petrov–Galerkin projection with W^T = (V^T P V)^{-1} V^T P); Thm. 2 and Lemma 3 (preservation); Sec. III-B/C, Thm. 3–4 and Alg. 1 (ISRK-style tangential H2 iteration); Eq. 14 and Fig. 5 (empirical error).
- **Verified mechanism:** This is offline projection-based reduction of the LTI block of a trained REN. The certificate P learned during training defines the left projector, which provably preserves the contraction rate and the (Q, S, R) incremental robustness (Thm. 2). The right projector is iterated toward H2-optimality conditions. **No output-error bound between the full and reduced models is derived.** The paper says outright that relating the H2 error to output trajectory errors is left for future work, and the accuracy evidence is empirical.
- **Overlap with H001:** Both use contraction of the state map as the basis of their guarantees and both target cheaper inference of a frozen recurrent model. A contracting system with incremental l2-gain gamma already bounds output deviation from bounded input or state perturbations, so H001's ledger can be read as a hand-rolled, mode-wise incremental-stability argument.
- **Remaining difference:** This is model reduction (fewer states, offline, input-independent), not online write selection. Its guarantee preserves properties of the reduced model, contraction and robustness, rather than bounding its distance to the full model. It has no per-step budget and no runtime measurement of skipped work.
- **What would eliminate the difference:** Work using REN or contraction incremental-gain certificates to bound the effect of runtime input or computation omission and drive a skipping policy. Event-triggered control on contracting systems is the most likely place to find this, and it was not searched in depth here.

## 6. DiffRNN: Differential Verification of Recurrent Neural Networks

- **Authors:** Sara Mohammadinejad, Brandon Paulsen, Chao Wang, Jyotirmoy V. Deshmukh
- **Version/date:** arXiv:2007.10135 v1, 2020-07-20. Published at FORMATS 2021 according to indexing sites found by search; I did not verify this on the publisher page.
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10135 (text read via https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.10135)
- **Access level:** full-text inspected (ar5iv rendering). A direct arXiv PDF fetch returned binary that could not be parsed, and the WebFetch tool cached it automatically. It was not opened or executed.
- **Relevant parts:** Sec. 2.1 (problem: certify |f(x) - f'(x)| <= epsilon for all x in region X); Sec. 4.1 (affine step: delta^a(t) from weight differences and delta^h(t-1)); Sec. 4.2 and Alg. 2 (Sigmoid/Tanh difference bounds, 12 cases); Alg. 3–4 (LSTM cell, differential evolution plus a dReal soundness check); Sec. 5 (16-bit weight-rounding benchmarks).
- **Verified mechanism:** For two structurally identical RNNs that differ only in weights, DiffRNN propagates interval bounds on the difference of pre-activations, hidden states and outputs step by step, starting from delta^h(0) = 0. The output is either "Proved" or "Unknown" for an epsilon over an input region. This is offline, pre-deployment verification evaluated on float32-to-float16 compression. It has no runtime monitoring and no compute-skipping policy, and the difference intervals become loose after a few steps for MNIST LSTMs.
- **Overlap with H001:** Both bound the output difference between a reference and an approximate execution of the same architecture, by propagating a per-step difference recursion through the recurrence. Both certify against the dense or original model rather than against ground truth.
- **Remaining difference:** DiffRNN's bound is offline and universally quantified over an input region, with SMT-checked bounds for nonlinear gates. H001's bound is computed online for the realized input, holds by construction (a linear recurrence with a contraction factor), and drives which computations to skip. DiffRNN has no pre-write bound and no budget.
- **What would eliminate the difference:** A differential-verification or runtime-assurance paper that runs the difference recursion online for the realized input and uses it to choose approximations. See the search hits below.

## 7. Targeted search hits (2–6 most relevant)

1. **An Exact Instrument for State Usage in Selective State-Space Models, and the Input-Driven Migration It Reveals.** R. Bhattacharya, arXiv:2607.11796, v1 2026-07-13, v2 2026-08-11. Access: abstract inspected only. *Mechanism:* the diagonal state gives an exact per-mode output decomposition, and a per-(layer, channel, window) Gram tensor gives the **exact** output error of dropping any mode subset. The paper finds that the useful modes migrate with the input, mostly through the input-dependent write map B_t, and that input-scheduled mode pruning beats static, Hankel and LAST-style rankings. Its scheduler "reads each window's mode usage from a first pass", and the author claims no deployed compute or memory saving. *Relevance:* this is the closest hit. It shows that input-dependent, write-driven, per-window mode selection with exact (non-causal) error accounting is already published. H001's distinct elements are that it is causal and single-pass, uses a pre-write upper bound instead of exact post-hoc error, always advances the homogeneous dynamics, guarantees per step, and measures runtime. **The methods should be read in full next; this lead is unresolved.**
2. **Delta Networks for Optimized Recurrent Network Computation.** Neil, Lee, Delbruck, Liu, arXiv:1612.05571, 2016. Access: full-text inspected (ar5iv). *Mechanism:* the network skips the column-wise matmul for input or hidden components whose change from a stored reference is below a fixed threshold Theta. The reference updates only on threshold crossing, which the paper says prevents error accumulation through drift. It reports 2x to 100x RNN-layer speedups and gives **no formal error bound**. The threshold is fixed and not budgeted per step. *Relevance:* this is the canonical online "skip the input write when it is cheap to skip" mechanism with measured runtime benefit. Its skip test (|Delta x_j| < Theta) is a cheap pre-matmul test, which is analogous in spirit to H001's beta.
3. **Skip RNN: Learning to Skip State Updates in Recurrent Neural Networks.** Campos et al., arXiv:1708.06834, ICLR 2018. Access: search snippets only, so this is an unresolved lead for detail. *Mechanism:* a learned binary gate skips the whole state update, copying the state forward. The compute budget enters only as a training-loss penalty, with no bound. *Relevance:* this is learned online skipping. Unlike H001, it freezes the state during a skip instead of advancing the homogeneous dynamics, and it carries no guarantee.
4. **A Deep State-Space Model Compression Method using Upper Bound on Output Error.** Sakamoto & Sato, arXiv:2510.14542, v1 2025-10-16, v3 2026-11-03. Access: abstract inspected. *Mechanism:* the paper derives an upper bound on the output error between two deep SSMs, with LQO blocks, in terms of the layerwise h2 errors, and minimizes it by gradient-based MOR. The bound appears to be offline. *Relevance:* this is another provable full-vs-reduced output-error bound for deep SSMs. It is static.
5. **Delta-SSM: Exact parallelization of spiking State Space Models.** Seghairi, Neftci, Bouhadjar, NeurIPS 2026 listing. Access: abstract inspected. *Mechanism:* event-driven integer-spiking Mamba-2, reported at about 45% of compute, with no error bound stated. *Relevance:* this is temporal-sparsity compute skipping in a modern SSM.
6. **Skip-Vision** (ICCV 2025 supplement), surfaced by search. It reports a cumulative per-layer error bound for skipping FFN computation on tokens, under a Lipschitz-type assumption, but in a VLM transformer rather than a recurrence. Access: search snippet only, so this is an unresolved lead.

No search hit combined all of the following: a cheap pre-write bound, a causal per-step ledger, an epsilon-feasible budget policy, and a measured runtime gain in an SSM. This absence is **not** evidence of novelty. Searches were standard-mode, few in number, and English-only. Event-triggered control and "self-triggered" or "lazy" linear system simulation literature was not covered and is the most likely home of an equivalent mechanism.

## Strongest argument that H001 is already covered by these sources

H001's guarantee is a superposition of known results in an obvious composition. For a diagonal linear SSM with a fixed readout, omitting an input write is exactly an additive input perturbation. Quamba (Thm. 4.1 / N.1) already writes the recursion Delta_t = A_t Delta_{t-1} + B delta_t and bounds it by a contraction-weighted geometric sum. LAST (App. B) and AIRE-Prune (App. B.5, Eq. 12–17) already bound the output effect of dropping modes of a diagonal SSM by sum_i ||C_i|| ||B_i|| / (1 - |lambda_i|) under a triangle inequality, with rho = max |lambda| as the worst-case factor. They also rank mode removals globally under a budget and, in AIRE's case, measure wall-clock speedup. Bhattacharya (2607.11796) has shown that input-dependent, write-driven mode selection beats static rankings and that its error is exactly computable per window. Delta Networks and Skip RNN have long done online, per-step, per-component write skipping with measured speedups, the former with a cheap pre-matmul magnitude test. A reviewer could argue that H001 just runs the Quamba-style recursion causally with the realized ||u_t|| and the gate in place of epsilon. In that framing, the LAST/AIRE per-mode gain becomes a per-step ledger, and greedy knapsack selection replaces a fixed threshold. Each step follows from standard results (the triangle inequality, ||W u|| <= ||W|| ||u||, and the triangle inequality on sum_i C_i h_i), so the certificate itself is unlikely to be judged novel. What is left to claim is (a) the causal, single-pass, budget-feasible scheduling with a guarantee that holds at every step, while the homogeneous dynamics keep running, and (b) an empirical demonstration that the bound is tight enough to skip a meaningful fraction of writes with real wall-clock gains. Point (b) is the part these sources do not supply.
